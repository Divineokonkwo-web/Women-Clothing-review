## Description

This project analyzes 23,465 customer reviews of a women's clothing catalogue to uncover patterns in customer sentiment, ratings, and recommendation behavior across departments, product classes, customer segments, and divisions. The analysis distills these patterns into actionable findings management can use to prioritize merchandising, quality, and marketing decisions.

## Business Introduction

The company needs a clear, data-driven view of how its women's clothing catalogue is performing with customers — beyond just an overall average rating. Understanding which departments, product classes, and divisions drive positive engagement (and which underperform) is critical for informed decisions on inventory, marketing spend, and quality control.

## Problem/Overview

While overall sentiment across the catalogue is strong — an average rating of 4.20 out of 5 — this headline number masks significant variation underneath. Certain product classes, like Trend, underperform on recommendation rate, while some classes generate almost no engagement at all. Without breaking the data down by department, class, division, and customer age, management has no way to identify where the real opportunities and problems lie.

## Aims of the Analysis

- Determine how star rating relates to recommendation likelihood
- Identify which departments and product classes drive the most engagement
- Identify underperforming or low-engagement product classes
- Compare sentiment across divisions
- Examine whether recommend rates vary meaningfully by customer age group

## Procedure

1. **Data Source:** Drew data from 23,465 customer reviews recorded in the Analysis sheet of the accompanying workbook
2. **Metric Calculation:** Computed average rating, total reviews, average customer age, and average positive feedback per review
3. **Segmentation Analysis:** Broke down ratings and recommendation rates by department, product class, division, and customer age group
4. **Pattern Identification:** Compared recommend rates and engagement volume across segments to identify standout and underperforming categories

## Insights

**Star Rating Strongly Predicts Recommendation**
Recommend rate climbs from just 1.9% at 1-star to 99.8% at 5-star, confirming rating is a dependable early-warning signal for dissatisfaction.

**Tops is the Standout Department**
Highest review volume (10,455), highest cumulative positive feedback (25,407), and the most recommendations (8,520) of any department.

**Engagement is Concentrated in a Few Classes**
Dresses, Knits, and Blouses drive the large majority of positive feedback, while Casual Bottoms, Chemises, and "Undefined" generate almost none — signalling either very low visibility or a data/categorisation gap.

**Trend Underperforms on Recommendation Rate**
At 73.9%, Trend trails every other class with meaningful volume by at least 6 points, pointing to a fit, quality, or expectation-setting problem specific to that line.

**Sentiment Varies by Division**
Initmates leads with 81.0% positive reviews, while General — the largest division by volume — lags at 76.7% and holds the largest absolute count of negative reviews (1,451).

**Recommend Rates are Stable Across Age Groups**
Recommend rates hold steady at 80–87% across age bands, with the youngest (18–24) and oldest (65+) customers slightly more likely to recommend than the middle bands.

## Recommendation

- **Launch a quality and fit review of the Trend class.** Its 73.9% recommend rate is a clear outlier versus category peers. Pull the underlying review text for Trend, audit sizing charts and product descriptions, and consider a supplier or QC review before the next buying cycle to close the gap with classes like Layering and Jeans (both above 88%)
- **Double down on Tops and resolve the low-engagement classes.** Prioritise inventory, marketing spend, and 4-5 star review testimonials around Tops, Dresses, Knits, and Blouses, where engagement is proven. In parallel, investigate Casual Bottoms, Chemises, and the "Undefined" class: confirm whether low engagement reflects genuinely weak demand (candidate for delisting) or a catalogue mis-tagging issue that is suppressing visibility and sales
